# Global Suicide Trends & Demographics Analysis (1979–2015)

## 📌 Project Overview
This project delivers a data-driven exploration of global suicide statistics based on official World Health Organization (WHO) records. Moving beyond simple charts, the analysis uncovers complex sociological patterns, gender disparities, and cultural impacts hidden behind the numbers. 

The core of the project is a clean, production-ready visualization dashboard that tells a cohesive story of how demographic factors intersect with global tragedies over a 36-year span.

## 🛠️ Tech Stack & Libraries
* **Python 3.12**
* **Pandas** & **NumPy** (Data cleaning, transformation, and multi-index aggregation)
* **Matplotlib** & **Seaborn** (Advanced grid layouts, custom color palettes, and polished data visualization)

## 📊 What’s Really Behind the Numbers: Main Insights

When you look at the final dashboard, the first thing that hits you is **how unevenly this pain is shared across the world**. Almost 40 years of data clearly show that we aren’t just looking at random numbers here—we are looking at deep social and cultural patterns. 

Here are the three main things the data is trying to tell us:

### 1. The Gender Gap: A Silent Crisis for Men
The global trend shows a massive, almost scary gulf between men and women. The lines run parallel, but at completely different heights. Men die by suicide way more often. 

This gets especially sharp on the risk chart, where the rate just skyrockets after the age of 75. It feels like in our world, men are catastrophically bad at opening up, or maybe they just feel like they aren't allowed to be weak. They bottle things up, deal with the stress alone, and when they face aging, losing their job status, or getting sick, they choose the darkest way out.

### 2. The Age "Pie" Paradox (Risk vs. Reality)
This is where the data pulls a really interesting trick on us. If you look at the risk levels, it seems like the oldest people ($75+$) are in the ultimate danger zone. But the moment you look at our donut charts, the whole picture shifts. 

The biggest slice of the pie (over $37\%$ for men and $32\%$ for women) actually belongs to people aged **35–54**. This is the prime, working-age population. Why? Simply because there are way more people alive in this age group. Plus, this is exactly when life hits hard: mid-life crises, career burnout, family dramas, and crazy financial pressure. These people are carrying the whole world on their shoulders, and sometimes, that weight just breaks them.

### 3. Geography and Religion: Cultural Immunity
The final breakdown by religion and the Top 10 countries is pure sociology in action. What people believe in and the kind of society they live in can literally keep them afloat—or push them over the edge.

* **Lithuania, Hungary, and the former Soviet bloc** (Russia, Ukraine, Belarus) at the very top of the charts are likely reflecting deep historical traumas, painful economic transitions, and a toxic culture of "drowning problems in alcohol," which hits men especially hard.
* **The Islamic world**, on the other hand, sits at the very bottom of the religion chart with the lowest rates. And it makes total sense. It’s not just about the religious fear of sin; it’s about how their society is built. In these cultures, a person is almost never left completely alone. Family, the religious community, and deep extended-family ties create a strong "social glue" that stops people from falling into the abyss.
